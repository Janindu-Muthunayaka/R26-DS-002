Why correction is needed at all

A sighted reader skims past a garbled word without noticing. A listener cannot. Text read aloud at word error rate 0.336 is not “slightly imperfect” — it breaks comprehension, and the listener has no way to go back and repair it visually.

The methodological point she will respect

The split is at photograph level, not sentence level. Sentences from one article share font, print quality, lighting and camera angle. Splitting by sentence would leak those conditions into the test set and inflate the result.

Why the spell checker makes things worse

Spell checkers assume phonetic or typing mistakes. OCR mistakes are visual. My most frequent confusion is ව → ච, a pair that looks almost identical but sounds nothing alike, so a lexicon has no reason to prefer it — and it replaces correct rare words with common ones instead. This is the argument that a dedicated model is necessary, not just nice to have.

Evidence · Analysis

What actually gets repaired

An error rate says how much a system improves text, not what it improves. I classified all 1,329 error operations in the test set using the Sinhala Unicode block, so every error falls in exactly one category.

Full-sequence mT5 Detector-gated (my proposal)
Be honest about this one

27.4% of all errors are formatting junk — stray underscores and rule-line fragments from photographing a page. The model removes 82.4% of those. On real Sinhala script errors alone it repairs 54.6%. Both numbers must be said; 54.6% is the honest measure of linguistic ability.

The finding that matters most

Dependent vowel signs are the largest category (299 errors, 22.5%) and the hardest to repair (50.8%). At newsprint size those marks are a few pixels. When OCR loses one, the evidence is gone from the text — the model can only guess from context. The ceiling here is optical, not statistical.

Proof that more data is not the answer

Growing the corpus from 38 to 230 photographed pages moved CER from 0.0763 to 0.0757 — a change well inside the confidence interval. More training text cannot restore information the camera never captured.

Evidence · The Negative Result

Why my proposed architecture lost

I designed a two-stage system: a SinBERT classifier flags the words containing errors, and mT5 corrects only those spans. The safety argument was sound — text that is not flagged cannot be damaged. It still lost, and I established why rather than guessing.

The reachability argument

A gated system can only repair what it flags, so detector recall is a hard ceiling on its fix rate:

η = 0.225 ÷ 0.656 = 0.34

On the spans it does flag, it repairs only a third of what is reachable. The full-sequence model repairs 61.8% of everything. So the loss is in the corrector, not the detector — a perfect detector would still lose.

It is also worse in all ten categories without exception, which rules out an unlucky error distribution.

The classifier is trained to find mistyped words. An underscore in the page margin is not a word at all, so it is never flagged, never reaches the corrector, and passes straight through. The full-sequence model regenerates the sentence, so the junk simply disappears.

The gated system does win one thing — say so

It destroys only 21 of 3,350 already-correct words (0.6%) against 111 (3.3%) for the full-sequence model — 5.5× safer. Its design intent is confirmed. It just does not buy enough correction to be worth it when the alternative delivered to a blind listener is raw OCR at WER 0.336.

Evidence · Method

The bug I found in my own evaluation

This is the part to volunteer before she asks. It turned a mistake into a contribution.

VersionWhat the alignment stage didRaw-OCR CER
v1 (discarded)Dropped pairs below a similarity threshold as “unalignable”0.0238
v2 (current)Keeps everything; reports the full distribution instead0.1197
 The gate removed roughly 57% of pairs — and specifically the hardest 57%, because similarity is lowest where OCR did worst. Raw OCR looked five times cleaner than it is.
How to tell it

“I caught it because 0.0238 is impossible for a photographed newspaper. I threw away every v1 number, rebuilt the evaluation, and locked the test set. Any post-OCR benchmark can make this mistake, so I report it as a finding: a paper should state what fraction of pairs its alignment stage discards, and on what criterion.”

Evidence · Capture Side

Two engineering findings that came free

Measured on the recognition input rather than on correction. Different unit system — item-level and normalised — so these numbers are never placed beside the ones above.

Combining several OCR passes only helps under one condition

Voting across five recognition views improved CER from 0.1754 to 0.1615 (+7.9%). The identical voting code applied to views of mixed quality made it 3.7% worse.

Voting assumes the systems fail independently. When the views form a quality gradient, the majority is dragged toward the weak ones. Comparable quality matters more than diverse configuration.

Capture and correction add up

Cleaner input → correction gets better, not redundant: 0.0649 on native input against 0.0365 on consensus input, 43.8% lower.
